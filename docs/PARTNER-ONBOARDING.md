# Partner onboarding

**Audience:** the partner team recruiting and verifying supply, and anyone writing partner-facing copy.

---

## Why a partner joins

Be honest in the pitch, because over-promising costs you the relationship in month two.

**What we actually offer:**

- **Matched work, not cold leads.** Only briefs that fit their services, area, capacity and calendar.
- **A back office.** Requests, quotations, calendar, bookings, customer history, earnings, payouts, reviews — replacing WhatsApp threads, phone notes and a spreadsheet. For most partners this is the real draw.
- **Written quotations with version history.** Nobody argues later about what was agreed.
- **Reliable settlement.** The platform collects; after delivery the payout is approved and settled with a statement.
- **No joining fee.** Commission on completed bookings only, agreed in writing beforehand.
- **A verified badge** that means something, because verification is real.

**What we do not offer, and should not imply:**

- Guaranteed volume
- Exclusivity
- Payment before delivery
- Control over which quote a customer picks

---

## Who to recruit first

Depth in a few categories beats thin coverage everywhere. For Dhaka, in priority order:

1. **Corporate caterers** — highest frequency, most repeatable, easiest to keep happy.
2. **Wedding caterers** — highest value per event.
3. **Decorators** — needed on most weddings and many corporate events.
4. **Photographers and videographers** — high attach rate, low operational risk.
5. **Sound, lighting and stage** — needed for conferences.
6. **Venues** — hardest to sign, most valuable once signed.

Target three to five verified partners per category per area before opening that area publicly. Fewer than three and you cannot offer a comparison, which is the product.

## The pipeline

```
Applied  →  Documents received  →  Under review  →  Approved  →  Published
                                            ↓
                                        Rejected
```

**1. Application** — through `/become-a-partner`, or entered by the team after a call. Creates the account, organisation and an unverified profile. They can sign in immediately but cannot receive briefs.

**2. Documents** — they send them or upload them; the team attaches them to the profile. Stored privately; only the partner and verification staff can open them.

**3. Review** — see the checklist below.

**4. Approve** — they start receiving invitations.

**5. Publish** — separate step. Only publish once the profile is good enough to sell: cover image, tagline, description, service areas, at least one package, realistic capacity.

## Verification checklist

| Document | Required for | Checking |
|---|---|---|
| Trade licence | Everyone | Current, matches the business name |
| Owner NID | Everyone | Matches the licence holder |
| Food licence | Caterers, kitchens | **Non-negotiable.** Do not approve a caterer without it |
| TIN certificate | Everyone | Needed for settlement and invoicing |
| BIN / VAT | If VAT-registered | Affects invoicing |
| Bank account proof | Everyone | Account name matches the business |
| Insurance | Large venues, structural builds | Where relevant |

**Beyond paperwork:**

- [ ] **Call two references.** Ask what was actually delivered, for how many, and whether anything went wrong. This is the single most useful check.
- [ ] **Visit the kitchen or premises** for caterers, if you can.
- [ ] **Sanity-check capacity.** A two-burner kitchen claiming 1500 guests is a future incident.
- [ ] **Sanity-check pricing.** Far below market usually means something is being left out.
- [ ] **Look at past work** — photos with real events, not stock images.

**When rejecting, be specific.** The reason is sent to them. "Food licence expired on 12 March — send the renewed copy and we will review again" is useful. "Application unsuccessful" is not, and loses a partner who might have been fine.

## Setting them up to succeed

An approved partner who never gets matched blames the platform. Get these right:

**1. Operating limits** — minimum order, guest range, lead time, daily capacity. These *directly* control which briefs reach them. Setting capacity optimistically produces a failed event, not more work.

**2. Service areas** — specific areas beat "all of Dhaka". Exact-area matches score higher.

**3. Categories** — only what they genuinely deliver themselves. A caterer who ticks "decoration" because they know someone will get matched to work they then sub-contract badly.

**4. At least one package** — gives customers something concrete and prefills their quote builder.

**5. Menus** — the biggest time-saver. The quote builder imports a whole menu in one click.

**6. Standard terms** — payment and cancellation terms prefill every quote.

**7. Cover image and tagline** — this is their listing. A default placeholder converts poorly.

## The first 30 days

**Week 1** — walk them through the dashboard live, on a call. Show the request inbox, the quote builder and the calendar. Make sure they can sign in on their phone; most partners will use it that way.

**Week 2** — send their first invitation, ideally something they will obviously win. Help them with the first quote so the format is right.

**Week 3–4** — review their response rate and quote quality. If they are slow, find out why: often notifications are going to an unmonitored address.

**Day 30** — review together. Invitations received, quotes submitted, bookings won. If they have won nothing, work out why: pricing, quote quality, or a shortlisting problem on our side.

## Keeping partners

| Signal | What it means | Do |
|---|---|---|
| Response rate falling | Disengaging, or notifications not reaching them | Call. Usually an email problem |
| Quoting but never winning | Priced high, or thin quotes | Show them an anonymised winning quote |
| Declining a lot | Wrong briefs | Fix their limits and areas |
| Rating falling | Real delivery problem | Address it early, before it costs them work |
| Payout query | Almost always a statement they did not understand | Walk through gross → commission → net |

**Pay on time.** Nothing loses partners faster than a late payout. A weekly batch, run reliably, is worth more than a favourable commission rate.

## Commission

Default 12%, overridable per partner in **Admin → Partners → Commission override** — which requires a written reason and is audited.

Reasonable reasons to vary it:

- **Launch partners** — a lower rate for the first few, in exchange for being a reference
- **High volume** — a tiered rate for partners delivering consistently
- **Low-margin categories** — venues and transport cannot carry catering's rate
- **Strategic supply** — a category with no other option

The rate must be agreed **in writing before they quote**. Changing it afterwards, even upward-looking, destroys trust and is visible to them in the dashboard.

---

## Suspending or removing a partner

**Suspend immediately** for: a food safety incident, fraud, serious misrepresentation of capacity, or abuse of customers or staff.

**Suspend after warning** for: repeated late cancellation, consistently poor ratings, persistent non-response, or attempts to move customers off-platform.

Suspension stops new invitations at once. **Confirmed bookings must still be delivered** — or you must find a replacement, which is the platform's obligation, not the customer's problem.

Record the reason. The partner sees it, and so will anyone reviewing the decision later.
